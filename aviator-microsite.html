The scenario

An asset history spread across three document types

Pickney Hills and Grand Boulee run overlapping fleets of rotating and electrical equipment. Each facility's maintenance team files its own condition reports and work records, in its own format, on its own schedule — the way most plants actually do it. Nothing here has been pre-organized into a single asset file.

The investigation

MTG-001 (Pickney Hills, Asset 4688): seven years, eleven documents, one recurring pattern

Read individually, each report looks routine. Read together, they show vibration climbing before both failures. This is the kind of pattern Aviator surfaces automatically when you ask it about an asset — expand each entry for what was actually found.

Aug 2018Green — baseline inspection, all readings nominal›
Rotor blades, stator windings, bearings, and seals all within normal limits. Bearing 1 vibration: 2.1 mm/s RMS. Establishes the healthy baseline everything after it is measured against.CR01_MTG-001
Nov 2018Yellow — vibration and insulation both drift out of range›
Bearing 1 vibration rises to 3.2 mm/s RMS; stator insulation resistance drops below the acceptable threshold on all three phases. Follow-up testing and a heat-exchanger cleaning are scheduled.CR02_MTG-001
Feb 2019Red — emergency shutdown, cracked rotor blades›
The Yellow trend from November progressed to visible cracks in multiple rotor blades, overheated stator insulation, and cavitation damage in the cooling system. Unit shut down; full rotor, stator, and bearing replacement ordered.CR03_MTG-001
May 2020Green — returned to service after full rebuild›
Rotor, stator windings, bearings, seals, and control software all replaced or renewed. Commissioning tests confirm the unit meets acceptance criteria before return to service.CR04_MTG-001
Aug 2020Yellow — early wear reappears three months post-rebuild›
Modest vibration increase and a seal beginning to leak. Still within limits, but the maintenance team schedules closer monitoring rather than waiting for the next routine cycle.CR05_MTG-001
Jun 2023Yellow — three-year vibration trend crosses a threshold›
Bearing 1 vibration has climbed from 2.0 to 2.7 mm/s RMS since the 2022 baseline; borescope inspection finds minor discoloration and deposits, but no cracking. Engineering flags possible foreign material introduced during a prior outage — a hypothesis that turns out to matter.CR12_MTG-001
Oct 2024Red — foreign object confirmed lodged in the turbine›
Vibration in the low-pressure section spikes again. Borescope inspection recovers a metallic object between rotor blades, consistent with the foreign-material hypothesis raised sixteen months earlier. Preliminary finding: it likely entered during a prior maintenance outage.CR15_MTG-001
Apr – Jun 2025Green — three consecutive clean maintenance records›
Following repair, Bearing 1 vibration trends back down (2.3 → 2.1 → 1.9 mm/s RMS across April and May), and June's inspection reports no findings at all — the asset stabilizes.MR_16543 · MR_23332 · MR_30908
What a document-by-document read would miss: the 2023 report that first raised "possible foreign material" (CR12) cites a 2022 baseline report, CR13 — but CR13 is filed under Grand Boulee, for a different physical turbine that only shares the name MTG-001. Aviator can catch a cross-reference like this because it resolves asset identity by facility and asset ID, not just by the label written on the page.

Investigate the way an engineer would ask

Instead of opening eleven PDFs, a reliability engineer asks Content Aviator directly. Every answer traces back to the report it came from.

Content Aviator — MTG-001 asset history
Has MTG-001 at Pickney Hills had vibration problems before the October 2024 shutdown?
Yes — twice. Bearing 1 vibration exceeded normal range in November 2018, which progressed to a rotor blade failure and emergency shutdown by February 2019. A second climb started around June 2023, when engineering suspected foreign material from a prior outage. That suspicion was confirmed in October 2024, when a borescope inspection found a metallic object lodged between the rotor blades.

Compare Bearing 1's current reading to the manual's action threshold.
The MTG manual sets the action threshold at 3.5 mm/s RMS, above which an in-depth spectral analysis is required. The latest maintenance record puts Bearing 1 at 1.9 mm/s RMS as of April 2025 — well within limits, and continuing the downward trend from the October 2024 repair.

Is CR12's reference to CR13 as a baseline actually valid?
Worth flagging: CR12 (Pickney Hills, Asset 4688) cites CR13 as its 2022 baseline, but CR13 is filed for a Main Turbine Generator at Grand Boulee, Asset ID 4600 — a different unit that shares only the MTG-001 name.
